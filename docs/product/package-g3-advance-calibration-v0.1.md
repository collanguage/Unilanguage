## Source-backed etymology

The consulted dictionaries support this chain:

> English `advance` ← Middle English `advauncen / avauncen` ← Anglo-French `avancer` ← Vulgar Latin `*abantiāre` ← Latin `abante` (“before, in front,” from `ab + ante`)

Merriam-Webster records `advancement` as an alteration of Middle English `avauncement`, borrowed from Anglo-French `avancement`, from `avancer + -ment`. CNRTL/TLFi records French `avance` as deverbal from `avancer` and `avancement` as `avancer + -ment`.

These sources do not support `ad.van.ce` or `ad.vance.ment` as historical segmentations, do not establish `vance` as an adjective meaning “前的,” and do not establish a historical relation with Chinese `往`.
