01

Literary Introduction · 文学引言

A child looks upward. Above him, the sky appears without walls, yet it gathers the whole world beneath it. A bird rises, a star seems distant, and a cloud passes from east to west. Before the child learns the words height, distance, or freedom, the body has already understood their directions.

一个孩子抬头望去。天空没有墙，却把整个世界收在它的下面。 鸟向上飞，星辰显得遥远，云从东方移向西方。在孩子学会“高度”、 “距离”或“自由”这些词以前，身体已经先理解了它们的方向。

Language begins in lived relations: above and below, inside and outside, near and far, toward and away. This theory chapter studies how these relations become semantic structure.

02

Definition · What Is Space?

In ordinary use, space may mean room, distance, an interval, an area, or the expanse beyond Earth. In this Protocol Book theory chapter, space is defined as:

Space is a relational field in which an entity, event, or idea can be located, oriented, bounded, separated, connected, or moved.

This definition has two layers:

Physical space
Observable relations among bodies and places: position, distance, direction, boundary, containment, and movement.
Semantic space
A structured field in which ideas are understood through relations such as high/low, inner/outer, near/far, center/edge, and source/path/goal.

The protocol does not claim that all meanings are spatial. It proposes that spatial relations provide one important and testable layer for constructing, comparing, and translating meaning.

Positioning statement · 定位说明

SPACE is not a named operation in the current Semantic Protocol. This chapter provides theoretical background for interpreting existing operations such as MOVE, CHANGE, EXIST, UP, DOWN, and CONNECT.

SPACE 不是当前 Semantic Protocol 中的一项正式操作。 本章是 Protocol Book 的理论章节，用于解释 MOVE、CHANGE、 EXIST、UP、DOWN 和 CONNECT 等现有语义操作所涉及的空间基础。

04

Etymology · 词源

English and French

English space entered through Old French espace, from Latin spatium, a word that carried senses including room, area, distance, and an interval of time. French espace belongs to the same historical line. The deeper origin of Latin spatium is uncertain.

Chinese

汉语“空间”由“空”与“间”组成。“空”可以表示未被占据、没有内容， 也可以指可容纳事物的空处；“间”突出事物之间的间隔与关系。 因而“空间”既可被理解为“可容纳之处”，也可被理解为“事物之间的关系场”。

Evidence boundary · 证据边界

The historical relation between English space and French espace is etymological. The comparison with Chinese 空间 is semantic and conceptual; this page does not claim historical cognacy between them.

Unilanguage Interpretation · 共通语言解释

Unilanguage treats space as a semantic coordinate system. A word or sentence may place an entity within a field, assign it a direction, establish a boundary, or describe a path of change.

For example, rise first describes upward movement. It can then describe a rise in temperature, status, hope, or intensity. The physical direction remains available as a semantic pattern even when no object literally moves.

Spatial metaphor

UP / 上

May organize meanings such as increase, emergence, visibility, power, or hope.

DOWN / 下

May organize meanings such as decrease, descent, submission, concealment, or sadness.

IN / 内

May organize meanings such as containment, belonging, privacy, memory, or inward attention.

OUT / 外

May organize meanings such as expression, exclusion, expansion, appearance, or release.

These associations are analytical possibilities, not fixed universal equations. Their strength must be tested in each language, expression, and cultural context.

The labels IN, OUT, TOWARD, and AWAY in this discussion describe spatial relations only. They are not being added to the current Semantic Protocol as formal operations.

06

Spatial Analysis Rules · 空间分析规则

The following rules guide analysis inside this theory chapter. They do not create a new SPACE operation or expand the official Semantic Protocol.

  1. Rule S1 — Position

    Meaning may locate an entity relative to a reference point: above, below, within, outside, before, behind, near, or far.

  2. Rule S2 — Direction

    A change of orientation can carry meaning. UP and DOWN are current Semantic Protocol operations. Inside, outside, toward, and away remain analytical spatial relations unless they are formally adopted in a later protocol version.

  3. Rule S3 — Boundary

    A boundary creates an inside and an outside. Crossing, opening, closing, entering, or leaving that boundary changes the semantic state.

  4. Rule S4 — Path

    Movement can be represented as SOURCE → PATH → GOAL. This structure supports both physical descriptions and abstract processes.

  5. Rule S5 — Scale and distance

    Near/far, large/small, center/edge, and open/closed relations can organize attention, importance, intimacy, accessibility, and difference.

  6. Rule S6 — Metaphorical projection

    A spatial relation may be projected onto a non-spatial domain. The translation should preserve the intended relation even when the target language uses a different image.

  7. Rule S7 — Context verification

    No spatial mapping should be accepted from form alone. It must be checked against syntax, usage, cultural context, and the speaker’s intended meaning.

Current research hypotheses

The project is also testing whether recurring sound patterns can interact with spatial meaning—for example, a as outward, i/u as downward, o as inward, and tonal movement as spatial movement.

These are Unilanguage research hypotheses, not universally proven linguistic laws. Each proposed mapping requires evidence, counterexamples, and cross-language testing.

07

Protocol Position · 空间在语义协议中的位置

Spatial Semantics is a theoretical chapter in the Protocol Book. It is not a node or operation in the current Semantic Protocol. Its role is to explain the spatial assumptions that may underlie existing operations and to provide an analytical bridge from those operations to word studies and translation experiments.

09

Reflection · 思考

When people say that hope rises, a thought comes from within, a problem lies ahead, or someone falls into despair, language turns invisible experience into a place that can be entered, crossed, shared, or escaped.

当我们说“希望升起”、“思想来自内心”、“问题就在前方”或“陷入绝望”时， 语言把看不见的经验变成了一个可以进入、穿越、分享或离开的地方。

This suggests a philosophical possibility: speech does not merely report a world already arranged. Through relations, boundaries, viewpoints, and directions, speech may also construct a temporary semantic world for speaker and listener to inhabit together.

This section is a philosophical interpretation. It is not presented as historical or experimental proof.
