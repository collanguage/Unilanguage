## CONTAINMENT

Freeze the English uncountable noun in its general restraint / prevention-of-spread sense. Collins distinguishes this from specialized uses and records contain + -ment as its formation. [Collins](https://www.collinsdictionary.com/dictionary/english/containment)

CONTAIN is the related verb, with both holding/including and restraining uses. Its Middle English–Old French–Latin lineage is lexical history. [American Heritage](https://www.ahdictionary.com/word/search.html?q=contain), [Oxford](https://www.oxfordlearnersdictionaries.com/definition/english/contain)

CONTAINER = entity; INSIDE = relation; ENCLOSE = operation; BOUNDARY = limit. This is the researcher's structural model, not a chronology or four etymological ancestors. A mathematical inclusion relation alone does not assert active restraint.

## LUMINOUS

The adjective permits emitted or reflected light / shining, as well as illuminated brightness. Its Latin source is luminosus; therefore previous concern that reflected brightness alone disqualifies 亮 is narrowed: it fits the brightness sense, but does not prove emission. [Collins luminous](https://www.collinsdictionary.com/dictionary/english/luminous)

LIGHT as a brightness noun or bright adjective has its own Old English lineage. Do not insert modern LIGHT as an ancestor of LUMINOUS. The weight adjective is a separate identity. A possible deeper European family connection is not a universal L rule and not Chinese common-origin evidence. [Collins light](https://www.collinsdictionary.com/dictionary/english/light)
