① Literary Introduction · 文学引言

“音与光本来就是一家，眼耳互换。”

《如光天籁》 repeatedly moves among sound, music, visible light and inner illumination. This passage is Literary / Cognitive Interpretation; it motivates a question but is not historical evidence. · 《如光天籁》反复在声音、音乐、可见之光与内在光明之间转换。此处属于文学／认知阐释，只提出问题，不构成历史证据。

② Basic Meaning · 基本词义

English: light, n. — visible illumination, brightness, or something that illuminates.

Chinese research mapping: 籁／籟 lài — historically a wind instrument, then sound, especially in 天籁 and 万籁.

④ Historical Etymology · 历史词源（独立层）

English light

light “brightness” ← Old English lēoht / līht ← Proto-Germanic *leuhtam / *leukhtam ← PIE *leuk- “light, brightness”. The English noun is inherited through Germanic, not borrowed from Latin. The “not heavy” homograph belongs to a different root and is excluded.

French and Latin

French lumière ← Christian Latin luminaria / luminare ← Latin lumen. Latin lux is a related member of the broader PIE *leuk- family, not an intermediate stage in lumière.

Chinese 籟／籁

從竹，賴聲；三孔龠也。 The historical path runs from a three-holed wind instrument to sound from apertures and broader sound expressions such as 人籟、地籟、天籟、萬籟.

Historical conclusion · 历史结论： no historical cognacy or borrowing is established between English light and Chinese 籟／籁.

⑤ Unilanguage Mapping · 共通语言映射

LIGHT ↔ SOUND / MUSIC

The modern forms share an approximate /laɪ/ sequence. English adds final /t/; Mandarin has a falling lexical tone and no final /t/. The direct lexical meanings differ, so the proposed relationship is phonetic and cross-modal, not translational or etymological. · 两个现代读音共享近似 /laɪ/ 序列；英语多词尾 /t/，普通话有去声而无词尾 /t/。直接词义不同，因此这里只提出语音与跨模态关系。
